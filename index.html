What it does

A map for people who find maps too busy

Easy Nav is a big-text map for everyone who finds the usual apps too busy, or too limited for how they really get around.

Calm and clear

A clean map in soft greys with only what matters. No ads, no clutter, big readable text. Highways, water and metro lines in gentle colours.

Easy to make your own

Save places, homes and your own routes in a few taps, and see them on the map the way you want.

Different languages available

Switch the language with one tap. Screens, place names and spoken directions all follow, and more languages are on the way.

Kind to every user

Large tap targets, clear words instead of icons alone, and nothing lost by accident: deleting always takes two taps.

Inside the app

Quiet, but never lifeless

Metro trains, water metro boats, buses, trains and autos move slowly along their lines. They stop, wait as if for people to board, then move on. They are only there to make the map feel alive, they step aside while you navigate, and you can turn them off.

Features

Helpful touches other map apps don't have

Small things, each one asked for by someone who got lost, or nearly did.

Search that forgives

Your saved places come first, then any place. Spelling slips and different spellings still find the right spot.

Places, sorted your way

Save favourites, points and spots, group favourites into categories, and set an alarm for a saved place.

Homes, one tap away

Go home in a tap. Keep more than one home, and hold and drag to put them in the order you like.

By car, bike or on foot

Time and kilometres at a glance, from where you are or from any saved place.

Practice drive

Watch the whole trip play out on the map before you set off, so the real one feels familiar.

Voice, or a soft alert

Hear every turn spoken, or choose a chime with a word or two. It keeps working with the screen off.

The map without internet

Save the map for 20 or 50 km around home. It quietly updates itself every month on Wi-Fi.

Light, dark or automatic

The whole map turns dark too. Switch between the calm street map and satellite whenever you like.

Trips worth looking back on

A summary at the end of each trip, and six months of trips, routes and badges, kept only on your phone.

Way to your door

The last two hundred metres

Map apps stop at the main gate, which is where most visitors get lost: a compound with eleven towers, a basement with three ramps, a lift nobody can find.

Mark the way once, from the main gate past the parking and lobby, up the lift to your door. Then send it as a message. Your guests open it and follow it step by step.

A shared way lasts 1, 7 or 30 days, as you choose. It lives on your phone, with no account and no server.

How it works

Three steps, then you are there

1

Pick where you're going

Tap a home, a saved place or a recent one, or search. Spelling slips are fine.

2

Practise, then go

Watch a practice drive if the way is new. Then follow one clear line, with the turn banner on top and the voice in your ear.

3

Arrive, really

It says you've arrived only when you're actually there, then ends the trip with a short summary.

Why it exists

It started with one wrong turn

My parents-in-law couldn't find their way to the hospital, and visiting relatives kept circling our apartment compound looking for the right tower. The map apps weren't wrong. They were just too busy to read while driving.

So Easy Nav got built, first for them, then for anyone who would rather be shown one road than offered forty. What works for elders works for everyone. It gets better every time someone says where they got lost.
