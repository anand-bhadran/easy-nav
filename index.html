What it does

Maps without the noise

No sponsored pins, no review badges, no twelve layers of detail. Large type, few choices, and a route you can follow at a glance — whether you drive here every day or have never seen the place.

Only what matters

Main roads and the places people actually ask for. Everything else stays off the screen.

Switch language in a tap

English and Malayalam today, with more as the map grows. Place names read the way people say them.

Starts where you stand

Picks up your location and shows the road to take. One button turns on spoken directions.

Routes drawn by hand

Hospitals and apartment compounds get a route someone walked first, not one guessed from satellite data.

Past the gate

The last two hundred metres

A normal map drops you at the entrance and stops. That is exactly where most people get lost — in a hospital block, a tech park, a gated compound with eleven towers and one visitor gate that is open.

Easy Nav keeps going: which gate, which tower, which lift, where to leave the car. Step by step, in plain words.

Why it exists

It started with one wrong turn

My parents-in-law could not find their way to the hospital, and visiting relatives kept circling our apartment compound looking for the right tower. The map apps were not wrong, only too busy to be read while driving.

So Easy Nav got built — first for them, then for anyone who would rather be shown one road than offered forty. It is a side project, built and tested in the open, and it gets better every time someone says where they got lost.

Try it, or tell us where you got lost

Testing is small and invite-based for now. Ask for a place to be mapped and it goes on the list.
